Listening to players: how feedback became updates

Devlog

2026-11-23

When the 1.22.8 update closed the browser chapter of UntoldLife on 15 November 2026, its first line said thank you to everyone who played, suggested ideas and reported problems, and that every update came from that feedback. It was not just politeness. If you read our update log from the bottom up, you can follow the conversation between players and the game almost day by day. Here is how it works, with real examples.

The ways players reach us

We wanted it to be easy to tell us something without leaving the game.

  • The feedback box inside the game. It shipped with the very first beta on 1 November. It is translated into every language the game supports, and all answers arrive in one place for us to read. You find it under «More».
  • Email. From 3 November (1.6.1) players can write to contact@369games.net.
  • Discord. On 15 November (1.22.7) we opened a Discord server for 369Games, with a link in the game and on our website. It is a place to chat with other players, share tips, suggest things and report bugs.

We also have anonymous play statistics, which tell us where players stop. They are not feedback in words, but they often say the same thing a message would, as you will see below.

The first big one: 1.7, «Your feedback became an update»

Three days after the beta, on 4 November, we gathered the first round of messages into one update and named it after them. It changed six things:

  • The greenhouse now protects 4 plots and can be upgraded up to 12, while the rest of the field follows the season.
  • What villagers like now changes every season, and someone who gets no gift for half a season becomes upset. Time away from the game does not count.
  • You can hold «Dig!» and «Cast!» instead of clicking again and again, and the new Worker's gloves at the smith raise the speed up to ten hits a second.
  • The market lists every item you have ever had, even at zero, so you can lock it before it comes in.
  • Legacy stars got a cap per generation, so one very long generation could not buy the whole tree.
  • The window that popped up when you caught a king fish was removed; it now shows in the event log only.

The last one is a good example of a small message with a clear answer. A popup that felt exciting to us the first time was an interruption to players the twentieth time.

Big systems players asked for

Some messages were too big for one patch. On 3 November we added a section to our roadmap called «big systems for later, from players' feedback». Two of its lines turned into whole parts of the game:

  • A better guild. The next day, 1.8 («The guild got stronger») added mercenary skills, guild ranks that decide how deep you can go, a pause between meals, and a preview of what each monster can drop. More guild updates followed through the week.
  • Crafting. Players wanted to collect materials like wool and iron and make things to sell. That became the Industrial Age, which opened on 8 November in 1.9. We wrote about it in an earlier post.

Small fixes with a name on them

Some of the most useful messages point at one small thing. When we can, we thank the player in the patch note.

  • 1.10, «Camels and your feedback» (5 November). Alongside the camels, the harvester, planter and sprinklers gained five levels of field coverage, tool heirlooms started giving gold, and ores lock themselves again with each new generation.
  • 1.13.1 (9 November). If all your animals had turned golden, you could not give gifts or hand in guild deliveries, because golden milk did not count as milk. Now golden products count, and the plain ones are used first. The patch note thanks Abu Mubarak for spotting it.
  • 1.17.1 (11 November). The «generations road» arrived in the Legacy tab: every stop from the second generation to the finale, with the next one in gold and the Industrial Age's conditions listed with your progress. The patch note thanks the player who suggested it.
  • 1.24 (18 November). A player suggested in the feedback box that the weekly challenge, village goals and leaderboards should have a place of their own. They now have the «This week» tab, with three new leaderboards. Its patch note ends with a thank you.

When the numbers speak instead of the players

Not every problem gets reported. People who leave in the first minute do not write to you. In mid-November our statistics showed that 66 of 243 players had opened the game and never started it. On a phone the first screen stayed empty for several seconds, then asked for a language, a name and a character before letting you in. 1.22.3 added a loading screen, moved the enter button up and made the rest optional. Nobody asked for it, but it was still feedback.

What we do with each message

There is no secret process. We read everything, and each message ends up in one of three places:

  1. Fixed right away if it is a bug and we can reproduce it. Many fixes went out the same day, such as 1.10.6, which restored contract blueprints a few hours after we broke them and gave everyone 15 blueprints to make up for it.
  2. Added to the roadmap if it is a bigger idea that fits the game.
  3. Left alone if it would make the game worse for most players. We try to explain these choices in the patch notes, as we did when we made legendary pet eggs drop only while the game is on screen (1.7.11).

One habit came out of this: every change gets its own version number and its own note, rather than being piled quietly into the last one. On 10 and 12 November we even split and merged patch notes so each one described one clear change. If a player asked for something, they should be able to find the day it arrived.

Where it goes from here

The browser version is now in maintenance mode: it stays playable, saves and leaderboards stay, and we fix anything that breaks. It is also where we try out ideas players name, as we did with the week tab. The bigger updates go to the PC version, which is coming to Steam. The feedback box, the email and the Discord server all stay open, and we are still reading.
