The factory: the Industrial Age

The factory is the second half of UntoldLife. It opens when three things are true: the family has reached its tenth generation, 80% of the legacy tree's levels have been bought, and you can pay 1,000 legacy stars. From then on the valley has two worlds, the village and the factory, and you move between them with one button.

Unlike the village, the factory does not restart when you retire. It belongs to the family.

Fields and workers

Four fields gather raw materials by themselves: Clay pit, Stone mountain, Forest, Sand quarry. A crew of up to 200 workers is shared between them, and you decide how many stand in each field. The sand quarry opens later, with the glass and steel it feeds.

Furnaces

Furnaces turn raw materials into building materials. Copper, iron and silver ore come from the village mine, and coal is the fuel for most recipes. When a furnace is short of something, the game marks it in red and names what is missing.

Your first hour in the factory

The factory starts tiny: one worker in the forest and room for 1,000 of each material. Everything is paid with village gold, and both worlds keep running while you look at the other one, even when the game is closed. A good opening:

  1. Hire workers. The first costs 50 million gold and each one costs about 5% more than the last. You can have 50 workers at first; each level of Workers' housing adds 15, up to 200.
  2. Buy a coal furnace and a cut stone furnace, then a brick furnace once you have coal.
  3. Raise the town hall early. Nothing in the factory can go above its level, and every hall level allows 3 more furnaces and benches of each kind (3 at level 0, 33 at level 10). Level 0 to 1 costs 500 million gold plus 400 wood and 400 stone.
  4. Raise the warehouse whenever a field shows "full". Each level doubles the room for every material.

The furnaces take copper, iron and silver straight from your village stock, so the game locks those three ores in the market. Keep the village miners busy; they now feed the factory too.

Balancing the chain: a worked example

Each worker gathers 0.5 of a raw material per second. Every field level adds +50%, and each level of the Richer fields upgrade adds +15%. Now look at the coal furnace: 3 wood in, 2 coal out, every 3 seconds. One coal furnace eats 1 wood per second, which is exactly two forest workers at field level 0, and it makes 0.67 coal per second.

A brick furnace (2 clay and 1 coal every 4 seconds) needs 0.5 clay and 0.25 coal per second: one clay worker and a bit over a third of a coal furnace. An iron ingot furnace (3 iron and 2 coal every 8 seconds) needs 0.375 iron and 0.25 coal per second. Coal goes into almost everything, so you usually want more coal furnaces than you think.

You need not do this by hand. The smart split button on the Fields tab puts as many workers on each field as your running furnaces and benches burn, and spreads the rest. Its automatic mode repeats this every 30 seconds. When a furnace stops, its card turns red and names the missing input.

New furnaces of the same kind get 30% dearer each, and field upgrades cost 200 million × 2.5 per level plus some of that field's own material. Upgrading a field is often cheaper than more workers once you have a few dozen.

Contracts: where the big gold is

The city tab shows 3 contracts at a time (4 with the Fourth trader patent). Each asks for one thing you already make, and two things after your third delivery. A contract pays 1.5 times the value of the goods, plus blueprints. Goods further down the chain are worth much more: a unit of clay is valued at 3 million, an iron ingot at 200 million, a gear at 1.6 billion, and a machine at 24 billion.

  • Every delivered contract makes the next ones 12% bigger, up to 300 contracts. They never ask for more than 90% of your warehouse.
  • An empty slot waits 10 minutes for a new contract. Each level of the Contract trader upgrade cuts 30 seconds, down to 5 minutes, and adds +10% contract gold.
  • You have 2 swaps for contracts you do not like. A used swap comes back after the same wait.

Contract gold counts as gold earned in the village. It helps your retirement goal, your legacy stars and the weekly gold board.

The engineer

Blueprints from contracts pay for five permanent upgrades with 10 levels each: Faster furnaces, Richer fields, Faster workshop (each +15% a level), Workers' housing and Contract trader. They cost no gold. The blueprint price per level goes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 13, 16, 20, plus planks and nails for the first three levels, gears and pipes for the next three, and machines for the last four.

The city: why the village gets cheaper

The Rails and the Boat are built in the city. Each level makes village miners (rails) or fishers (boat) 9% cheaper, 90% at level 10, and slows how fast their price grows from ×1.45 to ×1.12 per hire. This is what lets the village reach 200 miners and 200 fishers. Rails at level 10 is also the condition for the first stage of the train.

Renewal and patents

Once the town hall reaches level 10 (the palace), you can renew the factory. It starts over, and you get patents:

10 + half the sum of the town hall, warehouse and field levels + 1 for every 20 contracts delivered since the last renewal.

Example: town hall 10, warehouse 10 and three fields at 10 make 50 levels, so 25 patents. Add 100 contracts for 5 more, and the base 10: 40 patents.

A renewal resets the fields, workers, furnaces and benches, deep mine drills, warehouse, town hall, materials, contracts and the engineer. It keeps the rails, boat, public services, inventions, train progress and the harbor. Since the rails and boat stay, the village keeps its discounts.

Which inventions first

The whole invention map costs 493 patents. A practical order:

  1. Public services (10): opens the power company, water and the tax office, which pay gold every second and are never reset.
  2. Strong foundation and Ready warehouse: after each renewal the town hall and warehouse start a level higher per level. Every renewal gets faster.
  3. Stronger grid to level 5 (+20% service gold each), which opens Thrifty factories (40): every furnace and bench makes the same output from half the materials. This is the biggest single upgrade.
  4. Clean coal (50): coal furnaces make 3 coal instead of 2.
  5. Engineer's notebook (20) so you stop rebuying the engineer, and Furnaces that stay to restart with furnaces already built.

The First Train, step by step

Train progress is never reset, so it is the goal that spans all your renewals.

  • Stage 1 needs Rails at level 10 and 5T gold.
  • Stage 2 needs the town hall at level 10 and 10T. It also opens the sand quarry, the glass and steel furnaces, three new workshop benches up to the steam engine, and the harbor.
  • Stages 3 and 4 need 60% and 80% of the invention map spent (296 and 395 patents).
  • Stage 5 needs the whole map (493) plus 50 more patents and 100T.

So in practice you will renew several times before the end. When the train leaves, the story ends, and the family keeps +20% factory speed and +10% village gold for good.

The deep mine and the harbor

The deep mine holds drills you point at one ore each: coal for the factory, or copper, iron or silver for the village stock. A drill makes 2 ores a second at level 1 and 30 at level 10. The first costs 100B gold and 100 machines, the second 500B and 500 machines, and the third needs steam engines. If coal or iron runs short, this is the fix.

The harbor opens with stage 2. Ships carry your goods on voyages of 1, 4, 8 or 12 real hours and come back with gold and foreign goods, which buy permanent upgrades. A voyage keeps going while the game is closed.

Common mistakes

  • Building many furnaces with no workers behind them. A furnace without inputs is wasted gold.
  • Forgetting the warehouse. A full material stops its field.
  • Selling village iron and silver by hand after unlocking them. The furnaces need them.
  • Renewing too early. A renewal at a low level pays few patents; aim for the palace with high field and warehouse levels.
