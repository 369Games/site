The guild and the dungeon

The guild is the fighting side of the valley. You go down the dungeon one floor at a time, and every few floors a boss guards the way. Fights run by themselves; your part is the gear you bring, the food you carry and when you choose to go deeper.

Guild quests give guild points, and points raise your rank. A higher rank opens deeper floors and better quests.

Gear

You carry six pieces of gear: helmet, amulet, sword, armor, shield and boots. All of them are upgraded at the smith with ore from the mine. The inn in the village doubles how fast you recover, and the kitchen makes food heal twice as much in battle.

Legendary pets

Deep in the dungeon you can find legendary eggs. An egg hatches into one of three companions, and each one strengthens a different side of your fighter as it grows from baby to adult.

  • 🐉 the dragon: attack
  • 🦄 the unicorn: defense
  • 🦅 the griffin: Power

How the difficulty label works

Open the dungeon panel and every floor carries a small label. It is not a guess. The game takes your attack, defense, health and hit speed, builds the monster of that floor, and compares two times: how long you would last, and how long it takes you to clear the floor. A normal floor is cleared after 5 kills. A boss floor (every 10th floor) is cleared after 1 kill. Your health fills up again each time a floor is cleared.

  • Easy 😎: you would last at least one and a half times as long as the whole floor takes.
  • Fair 👍: you last at least 80% of the floor. This is the sweet spot for steady play.
  • Hard ⚠️ bring food: you can beat one monster before it beats you, but not five in a row without eating.
  • Very hard 🔥 relies on food: you only last about a third of a single fight. This label only shows when you carry food; with no food, the same floor reads as Deadly.
  • Deadly ☠️ get stronger first: you will fall.

The ⚖️ A floor that fits button uses the same math: it moves you to the deepest opened floor that is still Fair or better.

When you fall, you drop back one floor and rest until your health is full, and auto-advance waits 3 minutes before it tries again. Auto-eat starts under 40% health, once every 10 seconds (less with Fast eating). It picks the cheapest food that fills the gap and never eats golden items. Lock anything you want to keep in the market.

Your rank decides how deep you can go

Guild rank puts a ceiling on the dungeon: rank F lets you open floors up to 15, E up to 30, D up to 50, C up to 75, B up to 100, A up to 150, and S has no limit. The only exception is that you can always open 15 floors past where this generation started.

In the first generation, retiring needs dungeon floor 30. Rank F stops you at about floor 15, so you must reach rank E (40 guild points) before you can retire. If your hero is stuck at floor 15 with an Easy label, the problem is not your sword. It is your rank.

Getting guild points fast

The board holds up to 4 quests. A new one appears every 40 seconds, and an unused one disappears after 8 minutes. There are two kinds:

  • Deliveries ask for items you have already seen, like crops, animal products or monster materials. You hand them in at once, they never fail, they do not take a quest slot, and they give 20 guild points (before bonuses).
  • Hunts last 45 seconds, 90 seconds, 3, 6 or 10 minutes. Their points are your rank's rate per 90 seconds times the length: F gives 6 per 90 seconds, then 10, 16, 26, 40, 60 and 80 at S.

Example: at rank F, a 10-minute hunt pays 6 × 600 ÷ 90 = 40 points, enough for rank E on its own. Long hunts are the best value when your chance is good.

The chance of a hunt is (your power ÷ the power it asks)², kept between 5% and 100%. A hunt asks for 50% to 150% of your own power. So with 1,000 power, a hunt asking 1,200 succeeds about 69% of the time, and one asking 800 or less always succeeds. A failed hunt gives nothing, so skip the long ones you will probably lose.

You start with one quest at a time. Each Mercenary partner in the village adds one (up to 3), and level 3 of the Quests skill adds one more. Beating a boss deeper than any you beat before also gives half its floor number in points: the floor 40 boss is worth 20.

Mercenary skills

You get 1 skill point for every 2 combat levels and 2 points for every guild rank. Level n of a skill costs n points, so taking one skill to level 5 costs 15 points in total. Skills start over with every generation.

  • Power: +10% attack and defense per level. The safest first pick.
  • Loot: raises the material chance from 35% by 8% a level. At level 5, half the drops come with an extra material.
  • Fast eating: the wait between bites drops from 10 seconds to 2, and the quest board refreshes faster.
  • Quests: hunts finish 10% faster and give 20% more guild points per level, plus one more quest slot at level 3.

With all four at level 5, Mastery opens: no top level, 2 points a level, +0.2% attack, defense and material chance each.

After rank S: titles

At rank S, guild points also fill a title you pick. Each of the ten titles needs 50,000 points and then stays in the family, for example Monster Hunter (+10% monster gold) or Tamer of Legends (+25% egg chance). Only the title next to your name gives its bonus.

Raising a legendary pet

Every cleared floor has a 0.08% chance to drop an egg. Eggs only drop while the game is open and on screen, never in time away. Grace raises the chance: it is multiplied by 1 + hearts ÷ 10, so twice as likely at 10 hearts. The egg hatches after 30 minutes.

A pet gives +10% as a baby, +25% as a teen (300 growth) and +50% as an adult (1,500 growth). Growth follows the difficulty label: an Easy floor gives nothing (1 point only on the deepest floor you have opened this generation), Fair gives 1, Hard 2, Very hard 4 and Deadly 6. So the quickest way to raise a pet is to fight Hard floors with plenty of food. Once you own all three pets, a new egg feeds your youngest pet 500 growth, and when all three are adults, an egg turns into 20 legacy stars.

The saddle costs 100 million gold once and stays in the family; moving it costs nothing. A saddled dragon carries you two floors per clear, a griffin doubles materials, and a unicorn lets three titles work at once.

The Forgotten Depths

Before generation 4 the dungeon stops at floor 100. After that it keeps going, and every boss past floor 100 gives a legacy star on top of its loot.

Common mistakes

  • Upgrading the sword while stuck at the rank ceiling. Read the lock message under the floor info first.
  • Growing a pet on Easy floors, which give it almost nothing.
  • Selling all your food, so Hard floors become a wall.
  • Skipping the inn (2,500) and the kitchen (8,000), which double resting speed and healing.
