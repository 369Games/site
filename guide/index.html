UntoldLife game guide

UntoldLife is a free idle farming game that runs in your browser. You inherit your grandfather's farm in Echo Valley, plant the first turnips by hand, and slowly turn one small field into a working valley: a barn, a mine, a lake, an adventurers' guild, a smith and a village of people who remember your family.

What sets it apart is the family line. When a generation reaches its goal you retire and hand everything to your child, who starts again with legacy stars, heirlooms and permanent perks earned by the generations before. After ten generations the valley is ready for its Industrial Age: a factory with fields, furnaces and workshops, public services for the village, and finally the First Train.

This guide is written by the people who make the game. Every table on these pages is produced from the game's own data, so the numbers you read here are the numbers you will see when you play.

The game at a glance

  • 24 crops across four seasons and a set of rare seeds, 7 animals, 18 fish and 8 ores.
  • 29 legacy perks in six branches, 12 heirlooms and 6 challenge generations.
  • 54 achievements, a weekly leaderboard and shared village goals.
  • Progress continues while the game is closed, for up to 12 hours (more with a legacy perk).
  • Available in Arabic, English, Russian and Chinese, on phones and computers, with no download and no sign-up.

How to use this guide

You do not need to read this guide from start to finish. UntoldLife opens up slowly, one tab and one generation at a time, and most pages here are about places you will not see on your first day. The best way to use the guide is to read the page for the stage you are at, play, and come back when the game opens something new. The tables are made from the game's own data, so you can use them to compare crops, animals or recipes while you play.

Inside the game there is also a step-by-step guide for the first minutes, short hints the first time you open each tab, and a «Generations road» in the Legacy tab that shows the next milestone and what it needs. This website guide is for when you want the whole picture or the exact numbers.

How the systems connect

Each place in the valley is easier to understand once you see what it feeds and what feeds it. Here is the short version.

  • The farm pays for everything. Crops are your first gold, and gold buys plots, seeds, animals, helpers and upgrades. Crops are also food that heals you in the dungeon, and gifts for the villagers.
  • The mine feeds the smith. Ores sell for gold, but their real job is upgrading tools. A better hoe and watering can speed up the farm, a better pickaxe digs deeper, the sword, armor and other gear make the dungeon easier, and the rod helps fishing. Deeper ores pay for better upgrades, so the two climb together.
  • The barn and the lake are steady income. Animals produce on a fixed timer that does not care about the season, so they carry you when your crops are out of season. Fish sell for gold or make gifts for villagers, and from the second generation fishers fish for you.
  • The smith opens the guild. Your first tool upgrade opens the guild. The dungeon gives gold, materials and experience, and how deep you get is half of what you need to retire.
  • The village makes it run without you. Its buildings harvest, replant, sell and water for you, and that work keeps going while the game is closed. Its people give lasting bonuses as their hearts rise.
  • Legacy turns one life into the next. The gold you earned, the dungeon floors you climbed and how deep you dug become legacy stars. Stars buy permanent perks, so the next generation starts stronger. This loop is the heart of the game.
  • «This week» adds a rhythm. The weekly challenge counts things you already do and pays +10% gold and experience for seven days. The village goals reward speed in crops, mining or fishing for the players who helped.
  • The factory leans on the village. Its furnaces use copper, iron and silver ore from the village mine. In return, the railway makes the village's miners cheaper and the boat does the same for fishers, the public services pay gold every second, and finishing the First Train adds 10% to all gold.

The shape of one generation

Most generations follow the same shape: start with a small field and the perks you own, get the field running with helpers as fast as you can, spread out to the barn and the mine, upgrade your tools at the smith, and push the dungeon until you reach the retirement goal. Finish the three generation tasks on the way for extra stars. Then retire, spend the stars, pick an heirloom and start again, faster. Each generation also opens something new: the lake in the second, rare seeds in the third, the Forgotten Depths in the fourth, marriage and challenges in the fifth, and the factory in the tenth.

When something in one place feels slow, the answer is often in another place: a slow field may need a smith upgrade, a stuck dungeon may need more food from the farm, and a slow generation may need a perk from the legacy tree.
