Generations and legacy

A life in UntoldLife is one generation. When it reaches its goal (gold earned and a dungeon floor) you retire from the Legacy tab. The farm, the gold and the tools stay with that life; what moves on is the family: legacy stars, heirlooms, and every perk the family has ever bought.

Legacy stars are earned from what the generation achieved. You spend them in the legacy tree below, and the perks are permanent. This is why each generation moves faster than the one before, and why retiring early in the first generations is usually right.

The gold goal grows ×1.7 a generation up to generation 10, ×1.3 up to 20, and ×1.1 after that. The floor goal is 30 plus 3 per generation after the third, but it is always at least 15 floors above where this generation started. That matters once Battle memory starts you deep. Remember that the dungeon also needs the right guild rank: floor 30 needs rank E.

Where legacy stars come from

The Legacy tab shows the stars you would get right now. They are built from three parts:

  • Gold: 3 × the square root of (gold earned ÷ 1 million). 1M gives 3 stars, 4M gives 6, 100M gives 30. Four times the gold only doubles the stars.
  • Dungeon: one star for every 3 floors you opened this generation, counted from floor 10 or from your start floor if that is higher.
  • Mine: one star for every 10 mine floors past floor 20, at most 500.

All three are multiplied by your star bonuses (Wisdom of generations, the Lucky amulet, the Lean years challenge reward and Blessing of the Valley). The total has a cap per generation: 500 stars up to generation 5, 900 up to 10, 1,500 up to 15, 2,400 up to 20, then 3,600. Wisdom of generations and the Lucky amulet raise the cap too, so they never hit a wall.

Generation tasks: the big early bonus

Every generation also draws three tasks, like "harvest a number of crops" or "reach a dungeon floor". Each finished task adds 5% of the star cap, and finishing all three adds 20% in total. This bonus sits on top of the cap. The counting tasks ask for 60% of what your last generation did, so they stay fair as you grow.

A worked example

Generation 1, cap 500. You retire the moment you can: 1M gold (3 stars), dungeon floor 30 (6.7 stars) and mine floor 50 (3 stars). That is 12 stars. But if you also finished all three generation tasks, you get 20% of 500 = 100 more, for 112 in total.

Now say you stay longer and reach 100M gold, floor 100 and mine floor 220: 30 + 30 + 20 = 80 stars, plus the same 100 from tasks. Staying a long time added 68 stars, while the three tasks alone were worth 100. The lesson for the first generations: always finish the three tasks, then retire. Do not grind gold. Its stars grow slowly.

When to retire

  • Retire when the tasks are done and the next few stars would take much longer than the last few. Gold is usually the first part to slow down.
  • Dungeon floors are the cheapest stars early: three floors, one star. If your hero still finds Fair floors, keep going a little.
  • Bosses past floor 100 (from generation 4) give a legacy star each when you beat them, straight away, on top of the retirement count.
  • Before you press the button, spend what you can. Gold, tools and buildings do not move on.

Spending stars: which perks first

A perk's price grows with each level. Rich soil, for example, costs 2, 4, 6, 9, 14 and so on, 368 stars for all ten levels. Most branches open their next perks when the first one reaches level 3, which costs only 12 stars. That makes the first three levels of each root perk very good value.

A sensible order for the first few retirements:

  1. Haggling to level 3: +30% gold from everything, and it opens Starting capital and Sound sleep.
  2. Grandfather's name to level 3, then The workers remember you level 1: every generation starts with the harvester and planter covering half the field.
  3. Warrior blood to level 3: +45% attack and health makes the floor goal easy, and it opens Battle memory.
  4. Sound sleep: time away counts up to 12 hours, plus 6 hours a level. Great if you play in short visits.
  5. Wisdom of generations (it needs Sound sleep at level 2) once you can afford it: +10% stars and +10% cap per level. It pays for itself.

Spend 10 levels inside one branch and its special skill opens for 25 stars, such as Dynamite (each cleared mine floor takes you down two) or Battle Companion (a helper at half your strength who also takes hunts). Soul twin costs 300.

Two rules that save stars. First, the perks that set up your start (extra plots, starting gold, workers, chickens, miners, starting floor, hearts, tools) apply at once if you buy them in the first 2 days of a generation. After that they wait for the next generation. Second, you can reset the whole tree once per generation and get every star back, so a wrong choice is never final.

Picking heirlooms

At retirement you choose one heirloom from 3 offers (4 or 5 with the Family vault perk). The offers are drawn once per generation, so closing the window will not change them. The six tool heirlooms go up to level 6: each level starts the tool one tier higher and adds +1% gold. The Old coin (+5% gold a level) and Lucky amulet (+5% stars and cap a level) go to level 20 and stay useful forever. If both are offered, the amulet is usually the better long-term pick.

What each generation opens

  • Generation 2: fishing at the lake.
  • Generation 3: rare crops.
  • Generation 4: the Forgotten Depths, past dungeon floor 100.
  • Generation 5: marriage and lifelong friends in the village, and challenge generations from your next retirement.
  • Generation 7: the Industrial Age card appears in the Legacy tab.
  • Generation 10: the factory can be opened.

Challenge generations, in detail

From generation 5, the retirement window lets you pick a challenge for the next life. Each win raises that challenge's level, and its reward stacks for good:

  • Endless winter: +15% crop growth per level.
  • No workers: +10% gold from all sources per level.
  • Warrior only: +10% attack and health per level.
  • Lean years: +10% legacy stars per level.
  • Slow time: +10% speed for all production per level.
  • Underground: +20% mining power and gems per level.

The goals grow with each win: gold goals ×4, the dungeon goal +10 floors and the mine goal +20 floors. Lean years is the strongest long-term pick, since more stars means everything else comes faster.

The road to the factory

To open the Industrial Age you need generation 10, 80% of the tree's levels (106 of the 132 levels, not counting Blessing of the Valley, which has no top), and 1,000 stars to spend. The 80% rule means you can leave the most expensive levels for later. Plan your last few retirements around both the level count and the 1,000 stars.
