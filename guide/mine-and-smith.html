The mine and the smith

The mine goes down floor by floor. Press «Dig» to break rocks yourself, or hire miners to dig for you. Every five rocks broken on your deepest floor take you one floor further down. Deeper ores are worth more, and you need them to upgrade your tools.

How deep you can go depends on your pickaxe. A basic pickaxe stops at floor 12; each upgrade pushes the limit further, and the best pickaxe has no limit at all.

How deep each pickaxe digs

The pickaxe has seven levels. Their floor limits are 12, 28, 48, 75, 105, 150, and the seventh has none.

The smith

The smith upgrades twelve tools. Each one has six upgrades, paid for with gold and the ore of that level, so the mine and the smith feed each other.

  • 🌾 Hoe
  • 🚿 Watering can
  • ⛏️ Pickaxe
  • 🗡️ Sword
  • 🛡️ Armor
  • ⛑️ Helmet
  • 🔰 Shield
  • 📿 Amulet
  • 👢 Boots
  • 🎣 Fishing rod
  • 🧤 Worker's gloves
  • 🐑 Shepherd's crook

At the top of the smith is the forge, where a fully upgraded tool can be forged into its legendary form. Every forging rolls a bonus between 5% and 100%, and you can forge again to roll a better one. The perfect 100% roll has a 0.5% chance, and the 300th try on a tool is guaranteed, so nobody is left unlucky forever. The game shows how many tries are left under the forge button.

How a rock breaks

Every rock has a hardness, and every hit takes some of it away. When the hits add up to the hardness, the rock breaks and drops ore. Rocks get harder the deeper you go, and the hardness grows faster than the floor number:

Hardness5133060109184349

At the start, one tap on «Dig!» hits for about 1, so a rock on floor 1 takes about five taps. The pickaxe adds 60% dig power per tier and each mining level adds 5%. These add up together, and legacy perks such as «Strong arms» (+30% per level) multiply on top. You can also hold the button down instead of tapping: it digs twice a second, and up to ten times a second with fully upgraded worker's gloves.

Only the rocks you break on your deepest floor move you down. Five of them take you one floor deeper, but never past what your pickaxe allows. The «Up» and «Down» buttons let you go back to an easier floor, but rocks there do not count toward going deeper.

Which ore drops where

On each floor you can find every ore from floor 1 down to that depth. The newest ore is the most common, and each older ore is a bit more than half as likely as the one after it:

  • Floors 1–9: copper only.
  • Floor 10: about 65% iron, 35% copper.
  • Floor 25: about 54% silver, 30% iron, 16% copper.
  • Floor 45: about 50% gold ore, 27% silver, 15% iron and 8% copper.

This has a handy use. If you need copper for an upgrade and you are deep down, it is rare there. Go back up to floors 1–9, where every rock is copper and breaks in a few hits.

Deeper rocks also drop more pieces at once. A rock gives 1 ore down to floor 39, then 2 from floor 40, 3 from floor 80, 4 from floor 120, and so on. The step at floor 40 is the nicest: the rock is barely harder than on floor 39 (52 against 50), but it gives twice as much ore.

Every rock also has a small chance to drop a gem worth 2,500: 0.4%, plus 0.01% for every floor. That is 0.5% on floor 10 and 1.4% on floor 100.

These costs are the same for all twelve tools: tier 1 always costs 500 gold and 20 copper, tier 2 costs 3,000 gold and 30 iron, and so on. Taking one tool all the way to adamantite costs 4,665,500 gold. Jack, the smith, takes 3% off both the gold and the ore for every heart he has for you, so his friendship pays for itself.

Miners

A miner costs 250 gold, and each next one costs 45% more: 250, 362, 526, 762, 1,105. Five miners cost 3,005 in total. Each miner adds 0.4 dig power every second, times all your mining bonuses, and keeps digging while the game is closed. On floor 1 a single miner breaks a rock about every 12 seconds. On floor 45 the same miner, with no bonuses, needs about 150 seconds, so miners need a better pickaxe and mining bonuses to keep up as you go deeper. You can hire up to 200.

Miners dig on the floor you are standing on. If you leave the mine on an easy floor to collect copper, they will collect copper too, and they will not move you deeper.

Which tool to upgrade first

  • Pickaxe: the only way deeper, and deeper means better ore. Upgrade it first.
  • Shepherd's crook: +50% animal production per tier. If chickens and cows pay most of your bills, this is the best 500 gold you can spend at the smith.
  • Hoe: +20% crop growth per tier.
  • Watering can: water gives ×2.25 instead of ×2 at the first tier, and the «Water all» button starts working.
  • Worker's gloves: faster digging and casting while you hold the button.
  • Sword, armor and the other gear: you need floor 30 of the dungeon to retire, so these cannot wait forever.

Ores and gems are locked in the market by default. «Sell all unlocked» and the shipping bin skip them, so you never sell the iron you were saving by mistake. Unlock one in the market when you really want to sell it.

The legendary forge, in numbers

The first forging of a tool costs 500 mithril and 1,000 adamantite. Every forging after that costs a tenth: 50 mithril and 100 adamantite. Jack's hearts lower it too. Low rolls are common: white is 5–29%, blue 30–59%, purple 60–99%, and gold is the 100% roll. A forged tool lasts for the generation; the new generation starts again. The count toward the guaranteed 300th try belongs to the family, so it is never lost. Late in the game, a one-time «auto-forge» upgrade for 1 billion gold can forge for you, five tries a second, until it reaches the roll you asked for.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the mine on a high floor after collecting copper, so the miners stop taking you deeper.
  • Unlocking ores to sell them, then waiting for them again at the smith.
  • Ignoring Jack. Every heart is 3% off every upgrade.
  • Stopping at floor 39. Floor 40 doubles the ore per rock.

First generation vs later generations

Generation 1. The mine opens once you have earned 1,000 gold, and the smith after 20 broken rocks. The first upgrade of any tool opens the guild. A realistic first run gets to the iron or silver pickaxe. Each time you retire, the mine adds stars: about one for every 10 floors you reached past floor 20. Floor 48 is worth almost 3 stars, and floor 150 about 13, before bonuses.

Later generations. Tools go back to basic each generation unless the family helps. The «Family pickaxe» heirloom starts you with at least a copper pickaxe. The «Heirloom Tools» perk passes every tool down one tier lower instead of back to zero. «Mine crew» starts you with two miners per level, «Strong arms» adds 30% mining and fishing power per level, «Jeweler's eye» raises the gem chance, and «Dynamite» takes you down two floors at a time. With an adamantite pickaxe there is no floor limit: orichalcum appears at floor 500 (50,000 gold each) and starmetal at floor 1,000 (250,000). They are only for selling, and they are not locked. In the Industrial Age, rails make each new miner cheaper, so very large crews become possible. See The factory.
